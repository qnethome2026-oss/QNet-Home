# Health / Eldercare / Assistive-Tech Hackathon Research (Aug 2024 – Aug 2026)

Research for QNet Home (Snapdragon Multiverse Hackathon, Aug 3–7 2026). Goal: understand what has already won in the fall-detection / eldercare / assistive-tech space, what's overdone, and what response/escalation UX actually impresses judges — so we can sharpen QNet Home's differentiation and technical choices.

---

## 1. MIT Hacking Medicine — GrandHack 2024 & 2025

- **GrandHack 2024** (April 19–21, MIT) ran a dedicated **Aging/Elderly Populations track**. A notable placer was **"Dia Patch"** (3rd place, Elderly category) — a wound-healing patch for diabetic ulcers, built by a nanotech + biosciences student team. Notably this is a *hardware/biotech* solution, not a monitoring/AI app — a reminder that "aging" tracks at med-hackathons often reward clinical/device innovation, not just software dashboards.
- **GrandHack 2025**: **HospiTwin**, a digital-twin simulation for ED (emergency department) bottlenecks, won the InterSystems "Best Use of Generative AI in Healthcare" challenge. Again, a systems/ops angle, not a consumer monitoring product.
- **Takeaway**: MIT Hacking Medicine judges (clinicians + VCs) reward projects that show deep understanding of a specific clinical workflow/pain point, not generic "AI watches you" demos. A pure fall-detector would likely read as thin to this crowd.
- Sources: [MIT Grand Hack 2024](https://grandhack.mit.edu/mit-grand-hack-2024/), [Tec de Monterrey 3rd place recap](https://conecta.tec.mx/en/news/mexico-city/institution/grand-hack-tec-students-win-3rd-place-mit-hackathon), [GrandHack 2025 Recap](https://hackingmedicine.mit.edu/events/grandhack-2025), [HospiTwin note](https://bioethics.hms.harvard.edu/news/mbe-students-win-2025-mit-hacking-medicine-hospitwin-project)

## 2. NUS Medicine HealthHack 2025 (Singapore) — "Silver Generation Healthcare" track

This is the single most relevant category found: an aging-focused track judged head-to-head against value-based-care and data-platform tracks.

- **🥇 Reflexion** — **non-intrusive cognitive assessment embedded in a bathroom mirror**, screening for cognitive decline as part of a daily routine (brushing teeth, etc.) rather than as a clinical test the senior has to sit for. This is the standout differentiation pattern: *ambient assessment disguised as a normal daily object*, so the senior never feels "monitored" or "tested."
- **🥈 Vitals Lab** — uses a **smartphone selfie camera** (rPPG-style remote photoplethysmography) to measure vital signs, paired with an AI chat agent for follow-up. Shows judges reward using ubiquitous hardware (phone camera) over bespoke sensors.
- **🥉 Neural Drive** — thought-controlled wheelchair via brain-computer interface. High technical novelty but very hardware-heavy/low near-term feasibility — likely won on "wow" factor rather than deployability.
- **Ideas for QNet Home**:
  1. **Disguise the sensing as ambient/routine, not surveillance** — Reflexion's mirror trick is directly transferable: our Arduino UNO Q nodes already only emit semantic events, not video; we should lean into the "it's not a camera watching you, it's a smoke-detector-like sensor" framing in the pitch, mirroring how Reflexion reframed cognitive testing as "just brushing your teeth."
  2. Consider adding a **passive vitals/cognitive check-in angle** (e.g., voice-based "how are you feeling" during the daily conversational check-in could double as a light cognitive/mood screen) as a value-add beyond fall detection — this is a cheap software feature that mirrors a 1st-place-winning concept without new hardware.
- Sources: [HealthHack 2025 Winners Announcement](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/data/news-healthhack-2025-winners-announcement), [HealthHack 2025 recap](https://www.sginnovate.com/event/nus-medicine-healthhack-2025-0)

## 3. TreeHacks 2025 (Stanford) — Grand Prize: HawkWatch; Accessibility: BlinkAI

- **HawkWatch** won the **$11,000 Grand Prize** (1st place overall, largest collegiate hackathon prize pool, $200k total). It is a multi-camera surveillance dashboard that uses a **VLM (Gemini) to analyze live/recorded video streams** for crime and health emergencies (fainting, choking), auto-generates timestamped incident reports, and pushes phone/email alerts with an OpenAI chatbot for "what should I do" contextual guidance during an incident. Stack: Next.js/TypeScript frontend, Supabase, TensorFlow.js, Resend for notifications, WebSockets for real-time canvas overlays.
  - **Why it won**: not the AI model choice (off-the-shelf Gemini VLM) — it won on **polish, breadth (multi-camera + multi-incident-type), and a complete end-to-end demo loop** (detect → alert → in-context chatbot guidance) built in 36 hours. This is a strong signal: judges reward *finished, demoable loops* over novel-but-narrow tech.
  - **Caution**: HawkWatch streams and stores raw video to a VLM in the cloud — this is the OPPOSITE of our privacy-first, semantic-events-only architecture. This is a strong differentiation point we can make explicit in our pitch: "unlike typical hackathon camera-surveillance systems that ship raw video to a cloud VLM, QNet Home nodes never transmit video — only on-device-derived semantic events cross the network."
- **BlinkAI** won "Best Beginner" — translates eye blinks into Morse code into text into AI prompts, for people with ALS/Parkinson's who can't type or speak. Very narrow, single-modality accessibility hack — validates that judges reward accessibility ideas even when technically simple, if the human need is clear and demoed live (a team member actually blinking on stage).
- **Ideas for QNet Home**:
  1. Make sure our demo has the same "detect → semantic event → agent decision → voice response → tool call (light/notification)" loop fully closed and live-demoable, the way HawkWatch closed detect→alert→chatbot. A partial pipeline will lose to a smaller-scope-but-complete one.
  2. Explicitly contrast our "no raw video leaves the device" architecture against the common (and judge-recognizable) pattern of cloud-VLM-on-raw-video seen in HawkWatch-style entries — turn this into one of our loudest Technical Implementation talking points (bandwidth, energy, privacy, latency all improve when you send a 50-byte event instead of a video stream).
- Sources: [Stanford Daily TreeHacks 2025 recap](https://stanforddaily.com/2025/02/18/treehacks-awards-200000-in-prizes-to-students-from-around-the-world/), [HawkWatch Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/hawkwatch), [BlinkAI Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/blinkai)

## 4. PennApps HealthHacks+ — ALSistive & HeartStep

- **HeartStep** (2nd place) — a **"smart carpet"/bedroom mat** that passively monitors patient weight fluctuation to catch early signs of heart failure decompensation, pitched with real clinical stats (cites reductions of ~38% hospitalizations / ~56% mortality from compliant daily weight monitoring in heart-failure literature). Team: Gal Popovich & Naama Winetraub.
  - **Idea**: "Compliance-friendly passive sensing" (you don't have to remember to do anything, the sensor is just furniture) is a repeating winning pattern across multiple hackathons (Reflexion's mirror, HeartStep's mat). QNet Home's Arduino nodes are already passive/ambient — worth stating this pattern explicitly ("passive-by-design, zero caregiver/patient compliance burden") as a judged differentiator vs. wearables-based competitors.
  - **ALSistive** (1st place) — an ALS-focused assistive communication/control tool (details thin in public sources, but confirms ALS/motor-impairment assistive tools are a recurring winning category at student health hackathons — e.g., also BlinkAI at TreeHacks).
- Source: [HeartStep / Medibound writeup](https://medibound.com/stories/one-small-step-for-heartstep-one-giant-leap-for-heart-failure-detection)

## 5. ACL (HHS) Caregiver AI Prize Challenge — federal-level competition (2026, in progress)

- Run by the Administration for Community Living (part of HHS), this is a **$2.5M multi-phase federal prize competition** specifically targeting AI tools that ease the **family-caregiver crisis** (both unpaid family caregivers and the paid direct-care workforce). Phase 1 application deadline was **July 31, 2026**; Phase 1 winners announced **September 2026** — i.e., this is happening in parallel with our hackathon and signals where a lot of institutional money/attention is currently pointed: caregiver *burden relief*, not just patient monitoring.
- **Takeaway for pitch framing**: Judges/evaluators in this whole domain increasingly frame value not as "did we detect the fall" but as "did we reduce the cognitive/logistical/emotional load on the family caregiver." QNet Home should quantify caregiver load reduction (e.g., "caregiver gets a 1-line semantic status update and a reassurance summary instead of having to watch a live camera feed or field every worried phone call") as a first-class value metric, aligned with where the biggest funder in this space (HHS/ACL) is putting its money in 2026.
- Sources: [ACL Caregiver AI Prize Challenge](https://acl.gov/caregiver-ai-competition), [HHS press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/acl-launches-phase-1-caregiver-ai-prize-competition.html), [LeadingAge coverage](https://leadingage.org/hhs-caregiver-ai-prize-challenge-seeks-to-ease-caregiving-crisis/)

## 6. AARP AgeTech Collaborative — CES Pitch Challenges

- **ONSCREEN Inc.** won the **AARP AgeTech After Dark Pitch Challenge at CES 2024** ($10,000) for a **TV-based telehealth/AI caregiving platform** — i.e., using the TV (a device already in every senior's living room) as the interface, rather than requiring a new screen/app.
- AARP's own **2026 Tech Trends Report** shows AI adoption among 50+ adults nearly **doubled from 18% (2024) to 30% (2025)** — the market/judge appetite for "AI + aging" is measurably accelerating, which is good context for the pitch ("this is a now problem, not speculative").
- **Idea**: Use existing living-room hardware (TV/smart speaker) as the output surface for reassurance/companionship, the way ONSCREEN uses the TV — this is directly compatible with our plan to speak through "the room speaker," and we can cite the TV-interface precedent as validation that low-friction, no-new-screen UX is what wins caregiver-facing pitches.
- Sources: [ONSCREEN CES 2024 win](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/681493217/onscreen-wins-aarp-agetech-after-dark-pitch-challenge-at-ces-2024), [AARP AgeTech Collaborative at CES 2026](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-12-22-the-agetech-collaborative-from-aarp-leads-ces-2026-with-visionary-look-at-how-ai-will-shape-the-future-of-aging.html)

## 7. Assistive-tech hackathons for disability (deaf/blind/motor) — VOSAP & Huawei Spark

- **VOSAP International Assistive Technology Hackathon** — Top 10 winners spanned visual, hearing, locomotor, and learning disabilities; e.g. **E-Drishti**, a mobile app teaching shapes to visually-impaired users via a haptic/actuator hardware interface (touch-and-feel feedback). Pattern: winning assistive projects pair a *software AI layer* with a *cheap physical actuator/feedback channel* rather than software-only.
- **DeafTalk** won the grand prize at the **Huawei Cloud Spark Hackathon Singapore** (health-tech category) — an **Auditory-Verbal Therapy Assistant** for deaf/hard-of-hearing users, combining speech tech with therapy-protocol-aware AI (not just transcription — actual therapeutic scaffolding).
- **Idea**: Both winners succeeded by going one level deeper than "detect/transcribe" into "apply a known clinical/therapeutic protocol." QNet Home's fall-response could similarly cite a real protocol (e.g., mirroring **validation therapy** techniques used in dementia care, or standard fall first-response triage questions from geriatric EMS) rather than an ad-hoc "are you ok?" — small script change, adds clinical credibility judges reward.
- Sources: [VOSAP Top 10 Winners](https://www.voiceofsap.org/top-10-winners-vosap-assistive-tech-hackathon/), [DeafTalk / Huawei Spark](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/news/2022-first-place-huawei-hackathon)

## 8. Qualcomm's own hackathon series — direct precedent (same devices, same judges' ecosystem)

This is the most load-bearing research finding: **our own hackathon series has run multiple times already** with (per official pages) the *exact* device kit we have — Snapdragon AI PC, smartphone, Arduino UNO Q, AIC100 — under the name **"Snapdragon Multiverse Hackathon."** Confirmed prior/parallel instances: **Princeton (Sep 27–28, 2025)**, **MIT (Jan 24–25, 2026)**, **Columbia/CS3 (Feb 6–7, 2026)**, and **Bengaluru (2026)**. Public winner project write-ups were not indexed/discoverable for these specific instances (likely internal-only recaps), but the structure is confirmed: teams of 3–5, tracks = real-time computer vision / conversational AI / reinforcement-learning agents, Top Award (judged) + Team's Choice Award (peer-voted), prize = Meta Quest 3 per team member.
- **Related, separately-branded Qualcomm on-device AI hackathons** (useful as a proxy for what Qualcomm judges reward on this exact silicon):
  - **Windows on Snapdragon AI Hackathon** (Jan 13–Feb 24, 2025, $20k prizes, online) — build a Windows app using a Qualcomm AI Hub model; winner details not public, but confirms Qualcomm explicitly runs and prizes "Qualcomm AI Hub model on Snapdragon" categories — i.e., **using an official AI Hub model checkpoint is a legible, judge-recognizable technical choice** (worth confirming our SLM/VLM path uses/benchmarks against AI Hub if possible).
  - **On-device AI hackathon, Korea (Feb 2026)** — 5 winners, all running **NPU-resident** small/quantized models: **E.M.Pilot** (Qwen2-7B-Instruct + Nomic-Embed), **File Fairy** (LanceDB vector search), **emerGen** (Llama-3.2-3B + Whisper, emergency-response assistant), **Medly** (Qwen2.5-7B on Snapdragon X Elite NPU — translates medical jargon for patients, explicitly marketed as "runs entirely on-device via NPU for speed and security"), **MyStoryPal** (Llama-3.2-3B + Stable Diffusion 2.1, kids' English learning app).
  - **Takeaway**: Qualcomm judges consistently reward projects that can **name the exact model + explicitly say it runs on the NPU** (not GPU/CPU fallback) and connect that to speed/privacy/security. "Medly" is the closest analog health project and it won purely on the on-device-NPU health-translation angle — no fall detection, no monitoring, just a clean single-purpose on-device health tool. This confirms judges at *our own* hackathon series reward crisp on-device-AI framing over broad feature lists.
- **Ideas for QNet Home**:
  1. Name our exact models (e.g., which quantized SLM/VLM runs on the Copilot+ PC NPU, whisper/TTS models used) explicitly in the pitch and README, and state which stage runs on which device's NPU — this directly mirrors the pattern of every winning project in the Korea hackathon.
  2. Since this is literally the same hackathon franchise, treat "multi-device orchestration" (their stated theme) as the #1 grading axis, even above the individual AI features — Reinforcement-learning-agent / conversational-AI tracks explicitly exist, suggesting judges are primed to reward agentic tool-calling across devices (which matches our OpenClaw agent + tool-calling architecture well).
- Sources: [Qualcomm Snapdragon Multiverse Hackathon — MIT CSAIL](https://www.csail.mit.edu/event/snapdragon-multiverse-hackathon), [— Princeton](https://www.qualcomm.com/developer/events/snapdragon-multiverse-hackathon-princeton), [— Bangalore/Internshala](https://internshala.com/competitions/snapdragon-multiverse-hackathon-bengaluru/), [Windows on Snapdragon AI Hackathon Devpost](https://wos-ai.devpost.com/), [On-device AI hackathon Korea recap](https://www.qualcomm.com/developer/blog/2026/02/on-device-ai-developers-korea)

## 9. The "overdone" pattern: generic camera+MediaPipe fall detectors

Multiple independent hackathon submissions (spanning different schools/years) converge on an almost identical recipe: **webcam or IP-camera feed → Google MediaPipe/BlazePose pose estimation → simple classifier (MLP/heuristic) → SMS/push alert**, often with a Twilio or Firebase layer bolted on. Examples found:
- **NoFall** (TreeHacks 2021 — won "Most Technically Complex," "Best Use of Google Cloud," "Most Impressive Hack") — MediaPipe/BlazePose + Flask/React/Firebase + IFTTT-triggered voice risk assessments (Timed Up-and-Go / 30-second Chair Stand clinical tests) + SMS alerts.
- **SafetyNet** (HackDartmouth Virtual winner) — MediaPipe pose → MLP classifier, then **Google Cloud TTS asks "do you need help," STT + sentiment analysis interprets the reply, and a negative/no-response sentiment auto-escalates to 911 via Twilio.**
- **Fall Detection AI** (generic devpost entry) — MediaPipe pose estimation, alerts caregivers.
- **Ambianic.ai — "Remote Elderly Home Care via Privacy-Preserving Surveillance"** (originally a Pandemic Response Hackathon entry, now an open-source project) — this is the closest prior-art match to our architecture: **Raspberry Pi/NUC running local TensorFlow inference, WebRTC peer-to-peer (no cloud server ever sees the data), Vue.js PWA dashboard**, detecting falls/gas leaks/flooding. This validates that "local inference + no cloud + P2P alerting" is a known, respected pattern — but it has been around since ~2020, so a judge familiar with elder-tech may recognize the pattern and expect us to go further than Ambianic did.
- **Why this matters**: Camera + pose-estimation + push-notification fall detection is **thoroughly overdone** across at least 4+ independent hackathons/projects over the last 5 years, using nearly identical off-the-shelf components (MediaPipe/BlazePose). If QNet Home's demo narrative is "camera watches you, detects a fall, sends an alert," it will read as a known, dated pattern to any judge who has seen hackathon health tracks before.
- **How our seed idea already differs (validate/sharpen these in the pitch)**:
  1. **No video ever leaves the edge node** — SafetyNet/NoFall/HawkWatch all pipe frames or clips to a cloud API (Google Cloud, Gemini, Twilio); Ambianic keeps inference local but still frames it as "surveillance." QNet Home should frame the Arduino UNO Q nodes as **not cameras at all** in the pitch language — "semantic-event sensors," never say "camera feed."
  2. **Two-way agentic conversation, not just alert-and-forget**: SafetyNet's TTS/STT check-in is the closest existing precedent for our "Are you OK?" conversation loop, but it's a scripted one-shot Q&A with sentiment-analysis escalation, not a persistent local LLM agent that can converse, reason about context (which room, what happened before), and call multiple tools (lights, live-connect, notify, emergency). Our OpenClaw-based agent is a materially deeper capability than any of these projects demonstrated — emphasize the *agentic, multi-turn, multi-tool* nature explicitly, since "detect+alert" is table stakes and "agent that handles the whole incident like a person would" is not something these prior winners built.
  3. **Multi-device heterogeneous orchestration** is not present in ANY of the fall-detection precedents found — they are all single-device (one camera + one cloud backend, or one Pi). Our two Arduino nodes + Copilot+ PC + phone + AIC100 working as one distributed system is the genuinely uncommon axis, and it maps directly onto the hackathon's own explicit "multi-device AI orchestration" theme — lean hard into this rather than the fall-detection piece alone.
- Sources: [NoFall Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/nofall), [SafetyNet Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/safetynet-ucr2ij), [Fall Detection AI Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/fall-detector-ai), [Ambianic.ai Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/remote-elderly-home-care-via-privacy-preserving-surveillance)

## 10. Voice-cloning-for-caregiving is a live, funded idea — not novel; must be positioned carefully

**KindredMind** (real company, not a hackathon project, but the closest existing product to our "parent's cloned voice" twist): an AI phone-answering service for people with dementia that **answers repetitive/anxious calls in the family caregiver's own recorded voice**, built on validation-therapy principles (Naomi Feil) and simulated-presence-therapy research, with encrypted voice storage, a stated "not used to train external models" policy, a human failsafe that routes emergencies to the real caregiver, and membership in the Alzheimer's Foundation of America network. Founded by Kirstin Thomas after caring for her mother with frontotemporal/vascular dementia.
- **Why this matters for us**: our kid-monitoring "parent's cloned voice" feature is **not a blank-ocean idea** — a funded startup already ships almost exactly this concept for a different population (dementia patients calling in, vs. our kids being spoken to at home). Judges familiar with elder-tech news (Forbes covered "AI companions redefining elder care" in 2025) may know of KindredMind or similar. We should NOT pitch voice-cloning-for-family-communication as if we invented the concept; instead:
  1. **Differentiate the application**: KindredMind is reactive (answers a call *from* the patient); QNet Home is proactive/situational (the system *initiates* speech in the parent's voice at the moment of a detected event — fight, danger, TV-time violation — which is a materially different and arguably harder trigger-and-context problem).
  2. **Borrow their consent/safety framing wholesale** — it is well thought through and judge-legible: explicit opt-in "voice setup" via guided recording, encrypted storage, no third-party training use, and a human failsafe/escalation path. Building visibly similar consent UX (a clear "record your voice, with consent, revocable, encrypted, never leaves device" flow) will pre-empt the obvious ethical objection judges will raise about deepfake-adjacent tech, and shows technical/ethical maturity.
  3. Cite the **FTC Voice Cloning Challenge** (2024) in our documentation as evidence we're aware of the misuse-detection/authentication landscape around synthetic voice — it signals to judges that we've thought about the abuse case, not just the feature.
- Sources: [KindredMind](https://kindredmind.care/ai-companion-for-dementia), [Forbes: AI Companions Redefining Elder Care](https://www.forbes.com/sites/cathyrubin/2025/05/16/ai-companions-are-redefining-elder-care-3-ways-they-fight-loneliness-boost-safety-and-scale-support/), [FTC Voice Cloning Challenge winners](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-winners-voice-cloning-challenge)

## Ranked: Top ideas for QNet Home from this domain research

1. **Lead the pitch with "semantic events only, never video" as the headline technical/privacy differentiator, not a footnote.** Every fall-detection precedent found (NoFall, SafetyNet, HawkWatch, Fall Detection AI) ships raw frames/clips to a cloud CV/VLM API; even the privacy-conscious prior art (Ambianic) still calls itself "surveillance." Explicitly say "no camera feed ever leaves the room" and quantify it (bytes/event vs. video bitrate, latency, energy) — this is both a Technical Implementation (40 pts) and Use-Case/Innovation (25 pts) win against the entire genre.

2. **Reframe the pitch away from "fall detector" and toward "distributed incident-response agent across heterogeneous Snapdragon devices."** Fall detection alone is the single most repeated hackathon pattern found (4+ independent projects since 2020, all using MediaPipe/BlazePose). The genuinely rare axis — confirmed absent from every fall-detection precedent — is multi-device orchestration, which is also literally the stated theme of our own hackathon. Put "two Arduino nodes + PC agent + phone + AIC100 acting as one system" ahead of "we detect falls" in every slide.

3. **Name your models and which NPU runs them, explicitly, everywhere.** Every winning project in Qualcomm's own on-device-AI hackathon lineage (Medly, emerGen, E.M.Pilot in Korea; AI-Hub-model requirement in the Windows-on-Snapdragon hackathon) won partly by being crisp about "model X quantized, running on NPU Y, for reason Z (speed/privacy/battery)." Do the same for your SLM/VLM on the Copilot+ PC and any on-device model on the Arduino UNO Q / phone.

4. **Borrow KindredMind's consent/safety UX pattern wholesale for the parent-voice-cloning feature** — explicit guided voice-recording opt-in, encrypted local storage, "never used to train external models," and a hard human-failsafe/escalation path. This preempts the obvious "isn't this deepfake tech, ethically dicey" judge question and signals maturity. Also cite awareness of the FTC Voice Cloning Challenge in docs.

5. **Differentiate the voice-cloning trigger model from KindredMind explicitly in the pitch**: KindredMind is reactive (answers incoming calls from a dementia patient); QNet Home is proactive/contextual (system decides, from a situational assessment — danger, fighting, screen-time violation — to speak in the parent's voice, and lets the parent remotely inject a live message). State this contrast in one sentence in the deck so judges who know the space see we're aware of and beyond the existing product.

6. **Add one ambient, non-monitoring-feeling value-add feature, mirroring Reflexion's 1st-place "cognitive check disguised as brushing teeth" trick** — e.g., use the existing daily two-way "Are you OK?" conversation to also passively surface mood/cognitive/wellness signals over time, at zero extra hardware cost, framed as "check-in," never as "test" or "assessment."

7. **Script the fall/incident response around a named clinical protocol**, not an ad-hoc "are you ok?" (mirroring how DeafTalk grounded itself in real Auditory-Verbal Therapy protocol, and validation therapy grounds KindredMind). E.g., reference standard geriatric first-responder triage questions or a named de-escalation framework for the kid-monitoring case — a small documentation/script change that adds clinical/psychological credibility judges reward.

8. **Quantify caregiver cognitive-load reduction as a first-class metric**, not just detection accuracy/latency — this is where the biggest current funder in the space (HHS/ACL's $2.5M Caregiver AI Prize Challenge, live right now) is putting its money and attention in 2026. A slide like "caregiver receives 1 semantic summary instead of watching a live feed or fielding N worried calls/day" is a concrete, on-trend framing.

9. **Close the full detect→decide→speak→tool-call loop live in the demo**, the way Grand-Prize winner HawkWatch did (detect→alert→chatbot, in a browser, in 36 hours) — judges rewarded a complete, polished, narrow loop over a broader but partial one. Don't over-scope; make sure at least one end-to-end incident (e.g., simulated fall → agent reasoning → TTS reassurance → light flicker → caregiver notification) works flawlessly on stage.

10. **Use the room speaker / TV / existing living-room hardware as the interface**, not a new screen or app — directly validated by AARP CES-winner ONSCREEN's TV-based approach and generally by every "passive/ambient, zero new compliance burden" winning pattern (HeartStep's mat, Reflexion's mirror). This is already our plan; make it explicit as a UX/Deployment (20 pts) talking point — "no new device to learn, install, or remember to use."
