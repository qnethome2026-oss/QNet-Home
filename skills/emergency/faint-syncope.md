# Fainting (brief loss of consciousness)

**Trigger:** collapse event + person regains responsiveness within ~1 minute, OR person says they feel faint/dizzy/"about to pass out".

## Steps

**If they feel faint but haven't fallen:**
1. "Sit down on the floor or lie down right now — don't try to walk to a chair."
2. "Lie flat. If you can, raise your legs up on the sofa/cushion."
3. Fresh air if easy (bystander opens a window); loosen tight clothing at the neck.

**If they fainted and came back:**
1. Keep them lying flat for a few minutes — standing up straight away commonly causes a second faint. "Stay down, no rush."
2. They fell when they fainted → run the [[fall-responsive]] injury check (head, hip, wrist).
3. No food or drink until fully recovered and sitting up steadily. Then water is fine.
4. Getting up: sit for one minute → stand with support ([[fall-responsive]] get-up coaching if unsteady).

**Red flags — treat as emergency (caregiver alert + advise {emergency_number}):**
- Unresponsive for more than ~1 minute → this is not a faint → [[fall-no-response]] step 3 (breathing check)
- Chest pain, palpitations/irregular heartbeat before or after
- Fainted during exertion
- Shortness of breath, severe headache, weakness on one side, or confusion that doesn't clear
- Known heart condition, pregnancy, or taking blood thinners (household [[med-schedule]])
- Second faint the same day

## Say this (Herald phrasing hints)

- Faint = usually benign and frightening; tone is "this is common, let's be careful" not alarm.
- Always name the reason for a red-flag escalation honestly: "Because you mentioned chest pain, I've let Sarah know."

## Escalate when (Guardian policy)

- Any red flag → caregiver alert, priority high.
- Clean recovery → log + mention in caregiver daily digest (fainting episodes are worth a doctor conversation even when benign).
