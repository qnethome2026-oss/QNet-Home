# Choking (adult)

**Trigger:** audio distress event (gagging/silence mid-meal), person or bystander says "choking", or kitchen keyword event.

## Assess first — can they cough or speak?

- **Coughing forcefully / can talk** → MILD. Do NOT slap their back or do thrusts. "Keep coughing — coughing is the strongest thing clearing it. I'm staying with you." Monitor until clear. Worsens → severe path.
- **Cannot breathe, cough is silent/weak, no voice, clutching throat** → SEVERE, act now.

## Severe — bystander present

1. "Call out that you're helping. Have someone call {emergency_number}" (or caregiver escalation + speaker call).
2. **5 back blows:** "Stand behind and slightly to the side. Support their chest with one hand, lean them forward. Hit hard between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand — 5 times."
3. **5 abdominal thrusts:** "Stand behind them, arms around the waist. Fist just above the belly button, thumb side in. Grab your fist with the other hand, pull sharply inward and upward — 5 times."
4. **Alternate 5 and 5** until the object comes out or they collapse.
5. **They go limp/unresponsive** → "Lower them to the floor" → [[unresponsive-not-breathing-cpr]] (compressions can dislodge the object; look in the mouth between sets, remove only what you can see).
6. Cleared but received thrusts → advise medical check (thrusts can injure); log + caregiver notification.

## Severe — person is alone

1. Escalate immediately (they cannot speak — treat non-response as confirmation).
2. Coach self-help: "Push your belly hard against a chair back or counter edge, just above your belly button. Again."
3. Keep the mic open; silence after distress → treat as collapse, responders already inbound.

## Say this (Herald phrasing hints)

- Assessment question first, always: "Can you cough?" — a spoken answer is itself the answer.
- Severe: drop all pleasantries. Imperatives only.

## Escalate when (Guardian policy)

- Severe path: always, immediately. Mild path: log; escalate if it lasts > 2 minutes or worsens.
