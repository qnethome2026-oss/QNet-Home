# Unresponsive, not breathing — hands-only CPR coaching

**Trigger:** branch from [[fall-no-response]] or [[choking]] — bystander confirms no normal breathing (gasping counts as NOT breathing).

**Precondition:** a bystander is present. If the person is alone, this skill cannot help them — escalation ([[fall-no-response]] step 5) is everything.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the call is made:** "{emergency_number} first — is someone on the line? Put it on speaker." (Dispatcher instructions override this coaching.)
2. **Position:** "Move them onto their back on the floor. Kneel beside their chest."
3. **Hands:** "Put the heel of one hand on the center of the chest, on the breastbone between the nipples. Other hand on top, fingers laced."
4. **Compress:** "Push HARD and FAST — at least 2 inches deep, let the chest come all the way back up between pushes."
5. **Rate: 100–120 per minute.** Herald plays a 110 bpm metronome tone and counts aloud in sets of ten. ("Push with the beat. Don't stop.")
6. **Do not stop** except: the person moves/breathes normally, an AED is ready to analyze, a trained responder takes over, or the rescuer is too exhausted to continue. Interruptions cost survival — "You're doing it right. Keep going."
7. **If an AED is available** and a second bystander exists, send them for it: turn it on and follow its voice prompts — it will say when to stop touching the person. Resume compressions immediately after each shock/analysis.
8. **If they start breathing normally** and responders haven't arrived → [[unresponsive-breathing]] (recovery position), keep monitoring — be ready to restart compressions.

## Say this (Herald phrasing hints)

- Short imperative sentences, one at a time. Loud, steady, metronome-locked counting.
- Anticipate fear: "Ribs sometimes crack — keep going, that is survivable, stopping is not."
- Untrained rescuers do hands-only: do NOT coach rescue breaths.

## Escalate when (Guardian policy)

- Already escalated (this skill is only reachable post-escalation). Report "CPR in progress since «time»" to responders/caregiver.
